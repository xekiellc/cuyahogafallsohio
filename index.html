❄️ Summit County Snow Emergency Levels

Ohio uses a 3-level snow emergency system. New to Ohio? Understand this before your first winter.

1

Caution

Roads are hazardous. Drive carefully. Unnecessary travel is discouraged.

2

Emergency

Roads are hazardous. Only necessary travel. Motorists urged to stay home.

3

State of Emergency

All travel banned except emergencies. Violators subject to arrest.

Just Moved In?

New Resident Checklist

The stuff nobody tells you when you move to CF. Click each item as you complete it.

0 / 12 complete
  • Set up your CF Municipal Electric account — CF runs its own electric utility, not FirstEnergy. Call or visit City Hall to transfer service.
  • Find out your trash pickup day — it varies by neighborhood. See the schedule below.
  • Update your Ohio driver's license at the nearest BMV within 30 days of establishing residency.
  • Register your vehicle with the Summit County BMV. You'll need proof of Ohio insurance and your title.
  • Register to vote at your new address — Ohio requires registration at your current address.
  • Find a primary care physician — Summa Health and Cleveland Clinic both have offices throughout the CF/Akron area.
  • Locate your nearest urgent care before you need it.
  • Find a veterinarian if you have pets — see our pet services section.
  • Set up your water and sewer service with the City of Cuyahoga Falls.
  • Learn your snow emergency level rules — Level 3 means stay home or risk arrest.
  • Know where your nearest fire station is.

Straight Answers

Ask a Local

The questions newcomers always ask. Answered honestly — no spin.

CF is a mid-sized Ohio city — it has safe neighborhoods and areas you'd rather avoid, like anywhere else. The west side and areas near the gorge and riverwalk tend to be quiet and family-friendly. Do your homework using the neighborhood safety data above before buying or renting. Overall, CF is considered safer than many comparable Ohio cities its size.
The areas west of Route 8 toward the Bath Township line tend to be quiet, well-maintained, and popular with families. The neighborhoods near CVCA and the Silver Springs area also have a strong family feel. Use the crime and safety data above to compare specific streets before committing.
Arnie's West Branch for a proper steak night. Rocco's or Athena's if the pizza mood strikes. Chen's Garden for reliable Chinese. Das Schnitzel Haus when you want something nobody else does. El Meson for margaritas and solid Mexican. And Hartville Kitchen when you want a meal that reminds you why diners exist.
Cuyahoga Falls Municipal Electric has operated since the early 1900s — one of Ohio's municipal electric utilities. The city owns and operates its own distribution system, which historically has meant stable rates and local control. When your power goes out, you call CF directly — not a regional utility.
Real talk: northeast Ohio winters are serious. CF sits inland but close enough to Lake Erie to catch lake effect snow events. January and February can see multiple significant snow events. The gorge valley creates its own microclimate that can differ from Akron proper. Get good tires, learn the snow emergency level system, and keep your driveway clear — the neighbors notice.
Yes. CF offers more house for the money than most Cleveland suburbs, with shorter commutes to Akron than many alternatives. Private and parochial school options are strong. Access to outdoor recreation through the gorge and CVNP is genuinely exceptional. It's one of northeast Ohio's underrated places to plant roots.
Hike the gorge. Drive to Blossom for a show. Catch a RubberDucks game in Akron. Hit Cedar Point in the summer. Day-trip to Cleveland for a Browns or Guardians game. Explore CVNP year-round. There's more to do within 45 minutes of CF than most people realize when they first move here.
